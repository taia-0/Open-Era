# Game vision

## Project destination

Open Era is a hobby project with no deadline. It should become an original persistent maritime world inspired by themes of freedom, exploration, found family, extreme personal growth, and political upheaval. It must not depend on One Piece names, characters, geography, iconography, or other protected setting material.

The long-term server target is hundreds of active human players alongside hundreds of fully simulated named autonomous characters. Ordinary populations remain aggregated rather than becoming individual agents.

## Core promise

The world continues whether or not a particular player is online. Characters pursue their own ambitions, factions accumulate history, goods move physically, relationships change, and political dominance can persist indefinitely. There is no formal win screen: players define their own ambitions.

The most satisfying weekly outcome should be changing the balance of political power. That may mean winning office, protecting a settlement, creating a faction, breaking an alliance, enabling a rebellion, or helping an autonomous character become important—not merely accumulating personal combat power.

## Design pillars

### A simulated world, not a quest dispenser

Autonomous characters plan freely from their motives and knowledge. The simulation validates every proposed action and applies the consequences. Language-model text must never be allowed to bypass game rules.

### Human-scale asynchronous play

A short check-in should let the player:

- Review consequential events and intelligence.
- Issue delegated orders.
- Choose travel, exploration, or mission plans.
- Negotiate through persistent messages.
- Manage a crew, ships, territory, production, and essential resources.

Progress can benefit from other humans, but autonomous characters must be capable of filling every role. The world should remain playable when a player's allies are offline.

### Experimentation over tutorials

New players discover complexity by trying things with minimal instruction. Interfaces should reveal consequences, likely risks, and current intelligence without explaining the entire simulation up front.

### Political consequences without forced balance

A faction that becomes overwhelmingly powerful may remain dominant indefinitely. The simulation does not manufacture periodic resets or artificial rebellions solely to restore balance. Resistance must emerge from characters, material conditions, opportunities, and player action.

### Imperfect knowledge

Owned assets expose exact statistics. Foreign resources, colonization, defense, plans, and motives are estimates learned through observation, reports, behavior, dialogue, and investigation. Public news is searchable but intentionally incomplete.

## World and time

- Nearly everything advances continuously in real time.
- One real month equals one world year.
- Named characters may become less active when irrelevant, but they are not silently deleted.
- Human-controlled characters receive normal offline protection indefinitely.
- Political offices continue through autonomous deputies while their human officeholders are away.

## Presentation

The dominant presentation is an interactive top-down world map with illustrated panels. At normal zoom, the player sees a small region containing roughly two to four islands. Island and region names remain visible; details appear after selection.

The current visual direction is minimalist and tooney. Only islands and ships should normally appear as major sea interactions. Character illustrations use a chibi-anime treatment viewed from a three-quarter top-down angle.

## Success and failure

There is no universal end state. Success may be wealth, discovery, political office, protection of a home, a legendary crew, mastery of a power, faction leadership, or overthrow of the World Government.

Defeat should alter a story rather than erase it. Combat can cause wounds, troop loss, captivity, scattered followers, debt, reputational damage, and rare permanent loss of power. Defeat does not directly kill a named character; death enters through old age.

## Open product questions

- Which player audience should feel most at home remains undecided.
- The precise boundary between an accessible first hour and the intentionally deep simulation remains to be tested.
- How a claimed settlement is occupied afterward, and how its population responds, remains undecided. The prototype resolves the conquest itself with a garrison surrender threshold followed by an explicit claim decision, and the conquering character personally owns the captured settlement.
- The degree of offline protection in hostile territory needs explicit rules.
