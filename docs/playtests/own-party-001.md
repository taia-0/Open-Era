## Hypothesis and ambition

**Milestone hypothesis:** A party commander can run a profitable inter-island trade loop — buy a cargo cheap at one settlement, carry it to a distant island, sell it dear — using only the documented HTTP actions, while the party's provisioning state stays legible and survivable throughout.

**Player ambition:** As Mara Vane: load a cheap cargo at Crown Harbor, carry it to distant Cinder Key, sell it at a profit, and bring the party home intact.

**Success signal:** Ending money materially above the 108 starting money, with troops/sailors/health intact and no provisioning shortage.

## Outcome

From Mara Vane's perspective: she loaded cheap food at Crown Harbor and sailed the farthest known island run to Cinder Key, expecting a 2.7x price on the board she could see. The instant she arrived, the real price was 2.00 — the board figure had been a 16-tick-old rumour at 0.28 confidence, and the game had told her so on the party briefing before she left. The sale netted about 1.81/unit after a ~9% sell haircut, which does not cover five ticks of food burned on each leg. The expedition was a wash: the only money she made all session came from `work` (17–18.5 per tick), which out-earned the entire trade loop in two ticks.

Provisioning never endangered the party on the voyage itself: `runwayTicks` started at 62 and a round trip costs ten ticks of food. It only became lethal much later, when she deliberately idled at anchor with money in hand and the market alongside — and the game's only *push* warning arrived at one day of food remaining. She starved the party on purpose to measure that threshold, watched health floor at 91.4 and morale bleed to 73.6, then bought back food with the entire treasury in a single command, and finally rested the party back to health 100 / morale 80.6. She came home intact but not richer.

## Evidence review

- **World report:** Not player-visible. The public API exposes no world-summary surface; `briefing.items` is the nearest equivalent and covers only commands/intel/orders. Nothing in the API describes the world economy, only per-settlement `prices`/`stocks`.
- **Metrics:** Not player-visible. No endpoint exposes the recorded metrics; `events` contains `metrics-recorded` with `payloadWithheld: true` and no data.
- **Map:** The API exposes `settlements[].position {x,y}` and, once you are at a settlement, `travelTicks`/`travelDays` to each known settlement. There is no map view, no unknown-settlement discovery *for the commander* (all four settlements are in `characters[character-01].knowledge` from tick 0), and no way to see or price a settlement you have never heard of. Travel legs between two non-current settlements are not exposed (`travelTicks` is `null` unless you are standing there).
- **Decision/agency traces:** Fully player-visible. The event feed carries `player-command-accepted`, `player-action-executed`, `player-command-resolved` (with `outcome`) and `market-trade` events for the player, with `payloadWithheld: false`. However the default `GET /api/state` window (100 events) is flooded by world/NPC events — at this tick density 100 events covers well under one tick — so a player must page with `beforeSequence` to see their own trace. I only found these late; early on I trusted the `party` block and the briefing.
- **Conversation traces:** `conversations` = `{threads: [], messages: [], scheduledReplies: []}` for the entire session. I never found a documented way to open a conversation from `capabilities`, so this surface was dead weight.
- **Recovery and determinism:** Recovery is real and cheap: `rest` restores health (+4/tick) and morale (+3.1/tick) with no resource cost (`medicineUsed: 0`), even at a settlement; there is no passive health recovery and morale actively drifts down while working. Determinism was not tested with a second seed; prices drifted continuously per tick (crown-harbor provisions 1.43 → 1.88 → 2.12 → 2.41 → 4.50 across the session), so the economy is clearly stateful and path-dependent.

## Findings

### What worked

- **The `party` block is an excellent single surface for the milestone question.** From tick 0 it gives `provisions`, `demand`, `runwayTicks`, `runwayDays`, `shortage`, `healthPerTick`, `moralePerTick`, `troopLossPerTick` and a `resupply` sub-object (settlement, `exact`, stock, price, `travelTicks`, `reachable`). I never had to guess whether I could eat.
- **The critical warning is unusually honest.** `provision:critical` names the per-tick cost *before* you feel it ("That costs health 0.461 and morale 1.382 per tick"), names the settlement that can fix it, and explains why morale will not self-recover. That is exactly the text a panicking player needs.
- **`travel` is legible and safe.** `travel {fromId, toId, totalTicks, remainingTicks}` every tick, arrival is exact, and travel costs no extra food (`0.576/tick` whether sailing or idle). Party state was visible in transit at every tick.
- **Market intel has an explicit provenance ladder.** `intelligence {exact, source, confidence, observedTick, ageTicks}` and the `resupply.exact` flag make it mechanically clear when you are looking at a rumour. My failure was a judgement failure, not an information failure.
- **`work` is a reliable, always-available income floor** (17.3 at Crown Harbor, 18.5 at Cinder Key per tick) — the party never became unable to feed itself.

### Implementation defects

- **`trade-local` is an unlabelled black box.** It takes no parameters (no good, no quantity, no direction), returns only `{ok, command}`, and produces no event, no summary, and no message about what was traded. Across three calls it silently sold 5 `shipMaterials` (+17.84), then 13.6 provisions (+17.03), then 9.568 provisions (+17.34). The player cannot tell what it will liquidate, and on the second call it liquidated *food* I wanted to keep. Fix: return a result payload (good, units, unit price, proceeds, tax) and name the good/quantity.
- **`buy-provisions` has no quantity and can empty a treasury in one tick.** At tick 128 it converted my entire 112.83 balance into 32.13 provisions (avg ≈ 3.45, board price 2.4 → 4.5). Nothing in `capabilities` warns that the action scales to "as much as the hold and money allow."
- **The `insufficient-money` error contradicts the action's real cost.** `{"code":"insufficient-money","error":"Buying provisions costs 2 money; the character holds 0"}` implies a flat 2-money cost, while the identical action had just spent 112.83. The 2 is the *minimum balance* precondition, not the cost — the prose conflates them.
- **The cargo/provision capacity is never surfaced.** Provisions stop at exactly 47.424 (total cargo 54.424 with arms 3 + medicine 4 in the hold). Nothing in `capabilities`, the `party` block, or the character record announces a hold size; the only way to discover it is to spend money on buys that quietly purchase ~0.5 units. `buy-provisions` accepted at the cap still returns `ok: true` and still debits a fractional amount.
- **`moralePerTick` / `healthPerTick` are *only* starvation rates, not net rates.** The `party` block reported `moralePerTick 0` for the whole session while character morale fell ~0.3/tick from `work` (73.7 → 72.4 over four work ticks) and `healthPerTick 0` while health stayed pinned at 91.4 with no recovery. A player reading "0 per tick" reasonably concludes morale is stable. Rename to e.g. `shortageHealthPerTick`, or report true net deltas.
- **Minor:** the low-provisions copy reads "About 1 ticks (0.17 days) remain" — no singular handling.

### Design risks and opportunities

- **The trade loop as specified is not playable to profit.** The advertised ambition is "find a cargo you can buy cheap, carry it to a distant island, sell it dear." The documented action list allows buying **only provisions** (`buy-provisions`); arms/medicine/shipMaterials can never be purchased. The genuinely wide spreads that exist in the world (Cinder Key medicine 8.39 vs Glassport 4.07) are therefore unreachable. Meanwhile provisions prices across the three settlements I could price (1.74–2.00 at the same ticks) are tighter than the ~9–18% sell haircut plus the food burned in transit. Every route I could see was ≤ break-even.
- **`work` is degenerate relative to trade.** One `work` tick (17.33) ≈ the entire profit of the voyage's single sale (17.34), with no capital, no travel, no risk, and no stale-intel exposure. Two work ticks beat the whole ten-tick expedition. There is no reason for a profit-seeking commander to ever leave port.
- **Stale intel is the intended friction, but the failure mode is a trap rather than a decision.** The rumour was 3.94 against a true 2.00 — a 97% overstatement at 0.28 confidence. The player is only *reliably* corrected on arrival, i.e. after paying the round trip. `confidence` communicates uncertainty but not magnitude or direction; the briefing's staleness warning has no teeth. A "rumour vs. last known vs. faction report" price band, or letting `explore`/`trade-supplies` officers refresh a destination's price, would turn this into planning rather than a coin flip.
- **The provisioning warning is too late to be actionable for anyone not watching the panel.** `provision:low` fires at `runwayTicks <= 6` — under one day of food — and `provision:critical` at 0. The panel's continuous `runwayTicks` is good, but it is passive; the *push* surface behaves like a smoke alarm that sounds when the fire is already lit. A graded escalation (e.g. ≤20 ticks "plan a resupply", ≤6 "buy now") would match the excellent honesty of the critical copy.
- **Nothing auto-resupplies at anchor, even when the fix is on the doorstep.** The critical text says "Crown Harbor is alongside and sells provisions" while the party starves with `resupply.reachable: true`. That instruction-without-lever is the kind of thing players forgive once and resent later; a one-click "resupply from local stock" affordance (or a `directive`) is the obvious missing verb.
- **Market impact is large and undocumented.** Crown Harbor provisions went 1.43 → 4.50 over the session, with visible jumps immediately after my own purchases (2.12 → 2.41 after one buy). This is interesting depth, but with no quantity control and no cost preview the player is buying blind into a moving book.
- **The `party` block never explains *why* it is wrong.** During starvation I could see `shortage`, `healthPerTick`, `moralePerTick`, but nothing described *where* the food went or what would end it beyond the briefing line. The `character-upkeep` event payload (`demand, consumed, shortage, cargo, morale, health, troopCount, troopLoss`) is exactly the missing detail — and it is buried in an event stream the default window does not surface.
- **Surprisingly compelling:** the moment of arrival at Cinder Key and reading `source: "direct", confidence 1, price 2.00` was the best beat in the session — the game invalidated my plan using only information I already had. The provenance ladder is genuinely good design that deserves a stronger spotlight.

### Follow-up experiments

- Run the same ambition on a build where a general `buy-goods`/`sell-goods` pair exists, to test whether medicine/arms arbitrage produces a real trade loop, and whether the hold cap (54.4) is the intended profit ceiling.
- Measure `trade-local` systematically: hold a known, constant cargo and call it at each settlement in turn, recording the reported (if any) and realised proceeds to determine whether the haircut is tax, spread, or market impact.
- Probe the `provision:low` threshold across several party sizes and demands to see whether it is `runwayTicks <= 6` or a fixed provision count.
- Test whether `moralePerTick`/`healthPerTick` are literal net projections by raising morale loss (repeated `work`) and confirming the field stays 0.
- Explore whether an `issue-order` with an `explore` or `trade-supplies` directive can refresh a distant settlement's price intel, and whether officer trade income ever appears in the commander's money.

## Recommendation

`REVISE`

The party-provisioning half of this milestone is credibly done: the `party` block answers "how long can we keep going" from the first request, the starving brief is specific and actionable, and `rest`/`work`/resupply form a coherent loop that always leaves the player an out. The trade half is not done: of the four goods in the world only provisions can be bought, the buy action has no quantity and can drain an entire treasury in one tick, the sell action is an opaque parameterless auto-liquidation that will sell food you meant to keep, and every route the player can actually price is at or below break-even once the sell haircut and travel consumption are counted — so the stated ambition cannot be pursued, let alone achieved. Add a quantity-controlled, well-labelled buy/sell pair over all goods, surface the hold capacity and the true net per-tick morale/health deltas, move the low-provisions push warning earlier than one day, and re-run this exact seed/route before promoting.

## Response to this record

Added after the session, on the same branch. The commit under test above is `ef84d46`; these changes landed afterwards, so the observations above describe the build as it was played, not the build as it now stands.

Fixed, all three of them in this milestone's own surface:

- **`moralePerTick` and `healthPerTick` renamed to `shortageMoralePerTick` and `shortageHealthPerTick`.** This record is right that the old names were misleading rather than merely terse: the field reported the starvation term alone, so a fed party losing morale to `work` read `moralePerTick: 0` and reasonably concluded it was stable. The starvation cost is still what the briefing quotes, because that is the part the player is being asked to act on, but the names no longer claim to be a net projection.
- **The low-provisions alarm moved off one day of food.** It now fires when the hold could not cover a return voyage to the nearest market plus a four day reserve, which is the point at which the party can no longer guarantee its own exit rather than merely being low. On this seed and party that is `runwayTicks <= 24` instead of `<= 6`, so the warning this session first saw at tick 108 would have arrived around tick 84 and the one at tick 207 around tick 160. The reserve is a tuned constant, not a derivation, and it is marked as such in the source.
- **Singular handling for a one-tick runway**, and the briefing now also quotes the resupply ceiling.

One correction to the record's defect list. The hold does not cap at `47.424`; `47.424` is `48` minus one tick of upkeep. `buy-provisions` sells exactly the target `28 + troops * 0.25 = 48`, and because upkeep for the same tick lands after the purchase, the first read of the hold is `48 - 0.576`. The ceiling was genuinely invisible, which is the real defect, and it is now surfaced as `party.resupplyTarget` — computed by the same exported function the command charges against, so the quoted figure and the real one cannot drift apart. A test asserts the market sells exactly the quoted amount.
